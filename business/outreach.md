# Getting clients

## The flywheel

1. **Publish the workflows for free** (n8n template library, GitHub). Every listing ends with
   "Want this set up for you in a week? → [your link]". People who find a template but don't
   want to do the setup themselves become inbound leads.
2. **Run the pack on your own business.** Your own lead form gets an instant auto-reply, which is
   the first demo every prospect sees.
3. **Turn every client into a case study**: a screenshot of the digest, the before/after
   reply time, a short quote.
4. **Sell the care plan at handoff**, and ask for one referral at day 30.

## Pick one niche to start

Generic "I do automation" pitches get ignored. Choose one niche for the first 10 clients,
for example *home-service businesses with a website form* or *agencies that invoice net-30*.
Your examples, wording and case studies then all match their world.

## Where to find them

- Local business directories, Google Maps (search "[trade] near [city]") and owners'
  LinkedIn profiles. Look for a contact form and no instant reply: submit a real question
  and time the response. That is your opener.
- Bookkeepers and accountants: they see every client's overdue invoices. Offer a referral fee.
- Web designers: they build the forms but don't do back-office automation. Offer a 15% referral fee or a white-label rate.
- n8n community forum and Reddit threads where owners ask about chasing invoices. Help first, link second.

## Scripts

**Cold email (lead auto-reply angle)**

> Subject: your contact form
>
> Hi [Name], I sent a question through the form on [site] on Tuesday at 2pm and got a reply
> [the next morning / not yet]. Most people contact two or three businesses and go with whoever answers
> first.
>
> I set up instant, personal replies for [trade] businesses. The reply goes out within a minute,
> uses only facts you approve, and you get a text-style alert with the lead's details. It runs
> on your own accounts, and nothing goes live until you have seen it.
>
> Worth a 15-minute call this week? Or reply "show me" and I'll send a 2-minute video of it.
>
> [Your name]

**Cold email (invoice angle)**

> Subject: chasing invoices
>
> Hi [Name], quick question: who chases overdue invoices at [Business], and how often does it actually
> happen?
>
> I set up polite, automatic follow-ups for small businesses: a friendly note at 3 days, a firmer one at 10,
> a final one at 21, sent from your own Gmail and stopped the moment you mark an invoice paid. You get
> one summary email each morning instead of a spreadsheet to check.
>
> It runs in about a week, on your accounts. Open to a 15-minute call?

**Follow-up (3–4 days later)**

> Hi [Name], one more try. Here's what the morning summary looks like: [screenshot]. If it isn't a
> priority right now, no problem, I won't email again.

**LinkedIn / WhatsApp DM**

> Hi [Name], I help [niche] owners stop chasing invoices and answer every website lead within a
> minute, using automations that run on your own accounts. Would a 2-minute demo video be useful?

## Discovery call → proposal in 24 hours

Use the numbers the client gave you (see `offer.md`, "Why the price is easy to justify"),
recommend **one** package, and send the SOW the same day.
